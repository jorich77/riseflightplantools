Facebook groups are one of the richest sources of audience research. Your ideal clients are in there asking questions, venting, and sharing exactly what they need - for free.

👥
Facebook Groups
Best for: community research, warm outreach, direct engagement

How to find the right groups

  • Search your niche topic in the Facebook search bar - not your solution, their problem (e.g. "work from home mums" not "online business coaching")
  • Filter results by Groups and sort by Most Members
  • Look for groups with 1,000+ members AND recent daily activity - size without activity is useless
  • Join 5-10 groups and spend a week reading before you post anything
  • Note which groups feel like your people - the language, the questions, the level of engagement

What to look for once you're inside

  • The most-liked and most-commented posts - what topics generate the most engagement?
  • Questions people ask repeatedly - these are your content ideas and your offer topics
  • The exact words and phrases people use to describe their problem - use these in your content verbatim
  • Who gets the most helpful responses - are there potential collaborators or competitors to study?
  • Posts that get strong emotional reactions - frustration, relief, excitement - these map to pain points and desires

Search trick: In any Facebook group, use the search bar inside the group and search the word "help" or "struggling" or "how do I" - you'll find a goldmine of exactly what your audience needs that no one is properly serving.

How to show up in groups (without being spammy)

  • Spend the first week just commenting helpfully on other people's posts - add value before you promote anything
  • When you do post, ask questions rather than share content - "Anyone here tried X? What happened?" gets far more engagement
  • Share your story as a member, not as a business owner - vulnerability and honesty build faster trust than expertise
  • If the group allows it, share a relevant free resource occasionally - never lead with a sale

Instagram is where your audience scrolls, discovers, and decides who to follow and trust. Hashtag and account research here tells you exactly what content they engage with.

📱
Instagram
Best for: content research, competitor analysis, visual audience mapping

Hashtag research

  • Search hashtags related to your client's PROBLEM - not your solution (e.g. #workingmumlife, #corporateburnout, #mumwantingmore)
  • Look at the Top Posts tab - what content is getting the most saves and comments?
  • Check the size: hashtags with 10k-500k posts hit the sweet spot - big enough to have an audience, small enough to be visible
  • Save a list of 20-30 relevant hashtags across three categories: niche-specific, problem-specific, and identity-based

Account research

  • Find 5-10 accounts who serve a similar audience (not direct competitors - adjacent niches work well)
  • Read their comments - what are people asking? What are they sharing? What resonates most?
  • Look at their most-saved posts (high save count = content people want to refer back to - this is gold)
  • Notice what language their followers use - copy the phrases, not the content
  • Check who's following them - tap on a few followers and look at their profiles. Is that your person?

Comment mining: Go to a popular account in your space and filter comments by Most Relevant. People leave incredibly specific, honest reactions in comments. "I needed this because..." and "This is exactly what I've been struggling with..." are pure audience research.

What to document

  • The 5 posts with the highest comment count you've seen - what made people respond?
  • Recurring phrases your ideal clients use in comments
  • Content formats that get the most saves (talking head, carousel, text-only, etc.)
  • Gaps - what questions are being asked that no one is properly answering?

YouTube comments are the most honest, unfiltered audience research available. People write paragraphs in YouTube comments. They share their whole story. Mine them.

▶️
YouTube
Best for: deep pain point research, long-form content ideas, keyword discovery

How to use YouTube for research

  • Search your niche topic and filter by View Count - the most-viewed videos show you what your audience is most interested in
  • Read the comments on the top 5-10 videos in your space - sort by Top Comments
  • Look for comments that start with "I've been struggling with..." or "This helped me because..." or "What I really need is..."
  • Note which videos have the highest like-to-view ratio - this signals strong audience resonance
  • Check the "Questions" filter in comments if available - direct questions = direct content ideas

What to look for in comments

  • Personal stories - people share their exact situations in YouTube comments in ways they don't elsewhere
  • Frustrations with the creator's advice - "but what about..." comments tell you the gap in existing content
  • Requests - "can you make a video about..." is a direct brief for your content
  • Thank you comments that explain why the video helped - the specific detail in these is pure gold

Search trick: Type your niche topic followed by "for beginners" or "I don't know where to start" into YouTube search. The videos that come up - and their comments - map exactly to where your audience is right now.

Reddit is where people say what they actually think - anonymously, honestly, and in detail. It's the most unfiltered audience research tool available and almost no one in the coaching space uses it.

🔍
Reddit
Best for: raw, unfiltered pain point research - the truth your audience won't say on Instagram

How to find the right subreddits

  • Search Reddit for your audience's identity or situation - e.g. r/workingmoms, r/financialindependence, r/digitalnomad, r/sidehustle
  • Look for subreddits with 50k+ members and daily posts
  • Search within subreddits for "help", "advice", "how do I" - these threads are research gold
  • Sort by Top Posts (past year) to see the content that resonated most with that community

What to document

  • The exact words people use to describe their frustration - Reddit language is raw and specific
  • Common misconceptions - things people believe that are wrong, which you could correct with content
  • The questions that get 100+ comments - that level of response means it's a deeply felt problem
  • What advice people are giving each other - is it good? Is there a gap you could fill better?
  • Thread titles that made you immediately think "that's my person" - save them as content hook templates

Important: Reddit communities hate being sold to. Never post promotional content or links to your business in subreddits. Use this purely for research - the insight you gain will make your content and messaging 10x more resonant.

Your warmest audience is already in your phone. Before you spend a single hour on cold outreach or content creation, your existing network is your fastest and most underused research tool.

📞
Your Existing Network
Best for: fast validation, warm leads, real conversations

Who to reach out to

  • Former colleagues who you know are unhappy in their roles or who have expressed interest in doing something different
  • Friends who have mentioned wanting more flexibility, freedom, or income
  • People who have asked you how you do what you do
  • People in your WhatsApp groups who share content about side hustles, working from home, or lifestyle change
  • Anyone who has liked or commented on your content when you've talked about your own journey

How to have research conversations (not sales calls)

  • Frame it as research: "I'm building something and I'd love 20 minutes to understand your experience - not to sell you anything"
  • Ask what their biggest challenge is right now - and then ask "what's hard about that?" twice to get to the real answer
  • Ask what they've already tried and what didn't work - this tells you what the market is missing
  • Ask what their life would look like if this problem was solved - this is your marketing copy
  • Never pitch. End with "thank you, this is incredibly helpful." If they ask what you're building, tell them briefly and ask if they'd like to hear more.

The goal of these conversations: You are not selling. You are listening. The phrases people use to describe their problems - write them down verbatim. These become your hooks, your captions, and your sales page copy. Nobody describes your clients' problems better than your clients.

LinkedIn - your professional network

  • Search your niche keywords in LinkedIn Posts (not People) - see what professional content resonates in your space
  • Look at posts with high comment counts - what did people respond to and why?
  • Connect with people who match your ideal client profile and engage with their content genuinely before reaching out
  • Post about your own journey - LinkedIn audiences are often more engaged and responsive than Instagram for B2B adjacent niches

A WhatsApp message I can send to someone I know well asking for 20 minutes of their time for research\n2. A LinkedIn message for someone I know less well\n3. 5 questions I should ask in these conversations to get the most useful information\n\nTone: warm, honest, genuinely curious. No sales language at all.

Track your research as you go. Tick each task off when done and add a note on what you found. Bring this to your next coaching session.
