Objections

Every sales conversation follows the same five stages. Work through them in order - never jump to the pitch before you've built rapport.

1
Open - make a genuine connection

Comment on something specific from their content, stories, or bio. Not "love your page!" - something real that shows you actually looked.

Goal: get a reply. Nothing more. Don't mention your business yet.

2
Build rapport - ask about them

Ask one question about their situation. Listen to the answer. Ask a follow-up. People feel heard when you ask more than you say.

Goal: understand what they're struggling with. Let them tell you.

3
Identify the problem - dig deeper

Once they've shared a struggle, reflect it back. "It sounds like the main thing holding you back is X - is that right?" This shows you've listened and positions you as someone who gets it.

Goal: confirm the problem before you mention any solution.

4
Transition to a call - soft and natural

Only move here once you've confirmed there's a real problem and genuine interest. Frame the call as a conversation to help them, not a sales call.

Goal: get a call booked. Use your template below.

5
Confirm and prepare them

Once booked, send a short message the day before. Remind them of the time, what to expect, and ask them to think about one thing: what would change for them if this problem was solved?

Goal: make sure they show up primed and ready to engage.

These are starting points - not scripts. Personalise every single one. If it sounds like a template, it will feel like one.

Warm outreach - someone who engaged with your content Hey [name], I noticed you commented on my post about [topic] - really resonated with what you said about [their specific comment]. Are you in the middle of trying to [relevant goal] right now?
Cold outreach - someone who fits your ideal client profile Hi [name], I came across your profile and loved [something specific]. I work with [your niche] to help them [result you deliver]. Curious - is [specific problem] something you're working through at the moment?
Transition to a call It sounds like you're at exactly the point where a quick conversation could really help. I do a free [20/30]-minute call where we look at where you are, what's getting in the way, and what your next step could be - no pressure, just clarity. Would that be useful?
Call confirmation (send the day before) Looking forward to our call tomorrow at [time]. To get the most from it, have a think about this one question: if [the main problem we discussed] was sorted in the next 90 days, what would that change for you? See you then.
Story/content CTA (for followers watching your stories) If this resonates and you're wondering whether [your offer] could work for you, DM me the word [keyword] and I'll send you the details.

An objection is not a no. It's a question in disguise. Stay calm, acknowledge it, and redirect. Never argue.

"I can't afford it right now."
I completely understand - it's a real investment. Can I ask - if money wasn't the issue, would this be something you'd want to do? If yes: then let's talk about what's actually possible, because there may be options. If no: that's really useful to know - what would need to be different for it to feel right?
"I need to think about it."
Of course - it's a big decision and I'd never want you to rush. What specifically do you need to think through? Sometimes talking it out helps, and I'm happy to answer anything that's sitting with you.
"I don't have time right now."
That's exactly why most people come to me - they're already stretched. The question is: will you have more time in three months if nothing changes? This is designed to work around a busy life, not add to it.
"I've tried things like this before and it didn't work."
That's really important and I'm glad you said it - can you tell me what happened? What was missing? I'd rather understand what went wrong before than promise something that repeats it.
"I need to ask my partner first."
Completely makes sense - this affects both of you. Would it help if I put together a short summary of what's included and the investment so you have something clear to share with them?
"I'm not sure it'll work for me specifically."
That's a fair concern. Tell me more about your situation - what feels different about it? In my experience the people who worry most about whether it'll work are often the ones who get the most out of it, because they show up fully.
"Can you just tell me the price?"
I can - and I will. I just want to make sure I'm giving you the right number, because it depends on what level of support makes sense for where you are. Can I ask you one quick question first?
"I want to wait until [after summer / the new year / things calm down]."
I hear that - and I'd ask you gently: has there been a right time before? In my experience things don't calm down, they just change shape. What would actually need to be true for the time to feel right?
